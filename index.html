About

A little about me

I'm drawn to systems where many agents interact and the interesting behavior lives in the aggregate — markets, power grids, groups making decisions. A lot of my research is about building faithful simulations of those systems, then designing machine-learning methods that can tell honest signal from adversarial noise.

Right now, in the Strategic Reasoning Group at Michigan, that means modeling wash trading inside an agent-based limit-order-book simulator and building detectors that hold up when the adversary adapts. Earlier work took me through deep time-series forecasting and fairness-aware group decision-making. The thread connecting them is reliability: I care less about the headline accuracy number than about whether a method survives contact with the real, messy world.

My path here spans two continents: I studied applied statistics at Anhui University before transferring to Stony Brook to finish my B.S. in applied mathematics and statistics. I'm applying to CS PhD programs for Fall 2027, and outside of research you'll usually find me behind a camera or on a badminton court.

Selected research

in prep

Studying Financial Market Pathologies via Agent-Based Simulation

First author · Strategic Reasoning Group, University of Michigan · advised by Dr. Mithun Chakraborty · in preparation, 2026

Extended PyMarketSim with a wash-trading module built purely by subclassing the matching engine, generated 150k+ ground-truth-labeled simulations on the Great Lakes HPC cluster, and built a multi-channel detector that raises active-market recall 0.17 → 0.65 — then a Louvain community channel lifting recall against an adaptive adversary to 0.82 at 0.93 precision.

Code
published

A Coupled Framework for Power Load Forecasting with a Gaussian Implicit Spatio-Temporal Block and Attention Mechanisms

Co-author · Computers & Electrical Engineering, vol. 123, 2025 · Time Series Analytics Lab, Anhui University, with Prof. Jiaming Zhu

A VMD–Seq2Seq forecasting framework whose GIST blocks replace EM with a learnable GMM, cutting MAE / RMSE by 7.98% / 9.32% on 2M+ household records. Benchmarked against 16 baselines across 8 horizons, with gains confirmed by Diebold–Mariano tests at the 1% level.

Paper (DOI)
under review

A Fairness-Aware Cross-Efficiency DEA Framework for Large-Scale Group Decision-Making

First author · Decision Intelligence & Data Analytics Group, Anhui University · advised by Prof. Feifei Jin · under revision

FSC-DEA combines Probabilistic Linguistic Term Sets, Fehr–Schmidt fairness preferences, and cross-efficiency DEA. An iterative consensus algorithm derives decision-maker weights from pairwise similarity, raising group consensus from 0.62 to 0.85 with rankings robust to the fairness parameters.
